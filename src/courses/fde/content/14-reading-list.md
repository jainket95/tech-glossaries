## Discovery and requirements

| Book | Why it's here |
|---|---|
| **The Mom Test** — Rob Fitzpatrick | The single most directly useful book for chapter 3. Ask about specific, recent, real things people did — not hypotheticals. |
| **Inspired** — Marty Cagan | Written for PMs, but its discovery-before-solution approach transfers directly. |

## Trust, consulting, and client relationships

| Book | Why it's here |
|---|---|
| **The Trusted Advisor** — Maister, Green, Galford | The foundational book on what makes a client trust an outside advisor. Its trust equation is a direct lens on chapter 4. |
| **Difficult Conversations** — Stone, Patton, Heen (Harvard Negotiation Project) | For the hard moments in chapters 4 and 10 — bad news, pushback, a relationship that's gone sideways. |
| **Never Split the Difference** — Chris Voss | More intense than most FDE conversations warrant, but its calibrated-question technique applies to the harder ones. |

## Technical depth

| Book | Why it's here |
|---|---|
| **Designing Data-Intensive Applications** — Martin Kleppmann | The best single book for the technical judgment chapter 6 assumes — how real systems store, move, and silently corrupt data. |
| **The Phoenix Project** — Kim, Behr, Spafford | A novelized but accurate picture of how large IT orgs function under pressure — directly relevant to chapter 7. |

## Enterprise adoption

| Book | Why it's here |

| **Crossing the Chasm** — Geoffrey Moore | Why enterprise buyers are, correctly, more risk-averse than the standard adoption curve assumes. Background for chapters 4 and 7. |
